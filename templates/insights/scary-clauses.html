5 Scary Clauses Hiding in Free App Privacy Policies

Most people never read the privacy policy or terms of service. Companies know this — and they write the documents accordingly. Here are five common clauses that appear in free apps and websites, and why they should make you pause.

1. “We may share your data with third parties”

This often means your information can be sold or given to advertisers, data brokers, analytics partners, or other companies. Once the data leaves their systems, you usually lose almost all control over how it’s used.

2. “We are not responsible if your data is breached”

Many policies limit the company’s liability even if a breach happens because of weak security on their side. In plain English: if they get hacked and your data is exposed, they often try to avoid meaningful responsibility.

3. “You only receive a limited license — you don’t own anything”

You might think you’re downloading or buying software or content. In reality, you’re often just getting a temporary, revocable license. The company can change or take away access at any time.

4. “We can change these terms at any time”

They reserve the right to update the policy whenever they want. Sometimes they only need to post the new version on their website. Your continued use of the service is treated as automatic acceptance of the new terms.

5. “You waive the right to sue and must use arbitration”

Many free services force you into private arbitration and ban class-action lawsuits. This makes it much harder and more expensive for regular users to hold the company accountable.

These clauses are extremely common — especially in free tools, social apps, and freemium products.
