How to Read the Denial Reason on an EOB — and What to Do Next

The patient responsibility amount is often just the result. The denial reason on the EOB is usually the part that tells you whether the bill can still be fixed.

The EOB is the more useful document

A hospital or provider bill shows what they want to be paid. The Explanation of Benefits (EOB) shows what the insurer actually did with the claim: what was allowed, what was paid, and — most importantly — why something was denied or reduced.

Many people never look at the denial reason. They only see the patient responsibility line and assume the number is final. In a lot of cases it isn’t.

Common denial reasons and what they usually mean

  • Not a covered benefit / service not covered — The plan excludes this service entirely. Appealing is harder unless the exclusion was applied incorrectly.
  • Prior authorization required / not obtained — The service needed approval first. This is often fixable if the provider can submit the authorization after the fact or show it was an emergency.
  • Coding error / invalid code / unbundled — The claim was submitted with the wrong code or codes that should have been billed together. This is frequently corrected and resubmitted.
  • Eligibility / not eligible on date of service — Coverage wasn’t active, or the wrong member ID was used. Worth checking against your insurance card and the date of the visit.
  • Duplicate claim — The same service was already paid or is still processing. Compare dates and codes carefully.
  • Missing information — The insurer asked for more detail and didn’t get it. The provider can usually resubmit.

What to do next

  1. Find the denial reason code and the short description on the EOB (it’s usually in a remarks or reason column).
  2. Match the date of service and the code to the itemized bill from the provider.
  3. Call the insurer and ask them to explain the denial in plain language. Write down the reference number.
  4. Ask the provider’s billing office whether they plan to resubmit or appeal. Many coding and authorization issues are fixed on the second try.
  5. If the denial stands, check whether the No Surprises Act or your state’s rules limit what you can be billed.

Why this step matters

Paying the patient responsibility amount without checking the denial reason is how a lot of fixable claims become permanent charges. The EOB is the document that shows whether the number is based on a real coverage decision or on something that can still be corrected.
